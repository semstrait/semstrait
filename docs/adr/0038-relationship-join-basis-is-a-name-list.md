# A relationship declares its join basis under `on:` — as dimension names, or as `auto`, or as `cross`

> **Amended.** Later ADRs changed parts of this decision. Where they differ from the text below, the later ADR governs.
>
> - [ADR 0050](0050-a-relationship-is-compile-input-not-a-manifest-entity.md): supersedes the manifest half only: the `ResolvedJoinKey` semantic-id pair, the ordered-vector clause's manifest subject, and "`Field` stays reachable in the manifest for synthesised and derived cases" — which loses its relationship subject entirely, since an expression holds a computed key directly and needs no synthesised `Field`. 0038's authoring grammar (`on:` as names / `auto` / `cross`, the four join-basis rules, `on: []` as a parse error, `cross` + `filter` as the sanctioned non-equi join, cardinality conditional on the basis) is **untouched**. 0038's named rejected alternative is reversed, not beaten: 0038 rejected "retiring `JoinKeySide::Computed` along with the pair" on the ground that it is the landing site a later relationship-site expression grammar needs. The reversal is licensed by a fact that reading did not separate: `JoinKeySide` is a **private compile-internal** enum in `compile/seal.rs`, not a manifest carrier, so the persisted pair and the computed landing site were always two lanes. The landing site is not merely retired — it is made unnecessary, because a pooled expression *is* the relationship-site expression grammar's carrier. Its output type was already void besides: `resolve_key_side` returns a `SemanticMappingValue` that ADR 0037 retired, installed on a `SemanticBinding` that ADR 0043 retired.
> - [ADR 0052](0052-compile-gates-the-cardinality-it-materialises.md): its *Open questions* paragraph on cardinality — the open question — is **discharged**. Three clauses of it are now wrong rather than merely superseded, and are corrected in 0052's *Amends* rather than edited in 0038, on the precedent ADR 0040 set when it corrected ADR 0036's closing sentence in its own body: *"A compile-stage home is unreachable today"* is void, since ADR 0050 ratifies the stage; *"one gap is accepted rather than patched"* is **withdrawn**, and the gap closes; *"The real question is where cardinality's post-resolution invariants live"* is answered, and the plural is a singular. **0038's operative content stands untouched**: the `on:` grammar, cardinality's conditionality on the basis, the cardinality-matrix extension and its absent-cardinality row, the move of the missing-cardinality rule to validate, and the keyset evidence rule. In particular the matrix row keeps its `ManyToMany` and keeps allowing an authored value to override a default. 0052 catches a bad resolved pair; it does not re-derive the row that can produce one.
> - [ADR 0054](0054-compile-enumerates-every-legal-sub-composition.md): widens the `integrity` definition from "every `from` row finds a `to` row" to a claim that the composition is consistent, so an attested relationship filters neither side, as set out in 0054's *`integrity` claims the composition is consistent, so an attested relation filters neither side*. 0038's authoring grammar, its four join-key rules, its cardinality conditionality and its *"absence is never evidence"* principle are untouched — the last of these does not reach `Integrity::None`, which is a deliberate statement rather than an absence, since an author who writes nothing gets `Assumed`.
> - [ADR 0066](0066-the-plan-vocabulary-mirrors-substrait-v0-103-1-relations.md): the absent-cardinality row derives a cross join, which plans as a `CrossNode`, not inner; a preserved side on `on: cross` is no longer authorable (a LEFT theta join is unauthorable rather than unplannable); its "authorable and un-lowerable" disclosure is answered. All four cardinalities stay authorable beside `on: cross`, and an explicit symmetric one still owes `optional:` and `cross_filter:` — the owed `optional` is `none`, which is a value, so the completeness rule is satisfied without a preserved side. Its deferred `JoinNode.residual` landing site is `JoinNode.post_join_filter` under a keyed basis and a `FilterNode` above the `CrossNode` under `on: cross`. `JoinNode` has no cardinality field: the planner reads the resolved cardinality from the manifest to decide fan-out and writes it into the join's `semantic_meta`, in trace only.

## Context and Problem Statement

A relationship's join basis is a `{from: X, to: Y}` expression pair, and the pair form is what pushed authors into modelling one Semantics as two. How does a relationship declare its join basis instead, where are its rules enforced, and what follows for `cardinality` and keyset evidence?

## Considered Options

- `on:` as dimension names, `auto` or `cross` (chosen).
- **A sum-type element — bare name *or* a `{from, to}` pair, normalised at parse.** Preserves the asymmetric spelling, but it keeps two shapes alive through parse, validation and compile and needs a hand-rolled deserializer for the *entry*; a single element type needs none at all, and the pair form is what pushed authors into modelling one Semantics as two.
- **A fifth `Cardinality::Cartesian` variant.** Cardinality answers "how many rows match", which a conditionless join answers just as well as a keyed one — a broadcast against a single-row table is many-to-one. The variant would conflate *how the basis is formed* with *how many rows match*, duplicating on the cardinality axis a fact `on: cross` already states, and every one of the four existing variants would still be reachable.
- **Forcing `ManyToMany` on `on: cross`.** Would make the author state a false cardinality in the legitimate broadcast case, and would feed the fan-out rewrite and the adapter's `DISTINCT` posture a multiplicity the join does not have.
- **The `crossed` spelling.** `cross` is the operator's own name in every SQL dialect and reads as the join kind; `crossed` reads as a past-tense predicate about the relationship's state, and it is one keystroke of extra surface for no gain in clarity. Under `on:` the argument only strengthens: `on: cross` is character-for-character SQL's `CROSS JOIN`, which is what makes the key legible without a glossary, and `on: crossed` would throw that away.
- **`Option<Cardinality>`, with a door held open.** Recorded here as rejected, on the reasoning that holding a door is worse than either decision because it invites a `None` no consumer can honour. **Reversed** — see the Cardinality section. The bullet stays rather than being deleted, because what happened is a change of decision, not the discovery that the original was confused: the objection was to an unconditioned `None`, and the reversal supplies the condition and materialises the value before any consumer reads it.
- **Model-side enforcement of the four join-key rules.** Better locations, but the check would have to derive a composite DataKind's exposed-semantics set twice, since it does not exist before interfaces are built.
- **Keeping `on: []` as a validated state rather than a parse error.** Leaves two spellings for one meaning and makes the empty basis reachable by accident — the state most likely to produce a silent cartesian product would be the quietest to write.
- **Retiring `JoinKeySide::Computed` along with the pair.** It is the landing site a later relationship-site expression grammar needs, and every dormant lane is disclosed where it lives rather than left to be discovered.

## Decision Outcome

`Relationship.on` is a `JoinKeySpec` — `Declared(Vec<SemanticsName>)`, `Auto`, or `Cross` — and the `{from: X, to: Y}` expression pair retires.

The wire key is `on:`, the word SQL already uses for a join condition, which keeps the basis from reading as one more statement about identity in a model that also carries keysets, key dimensions and `is_key:`. `JoinKeySpec` keeps its name because it describes the value, not the key.

One name is one Semantics on both sides: `on: [a, b]` means `from.a = to.a AND from.b = to.b`. Column-level asymmetry is not lost, because the semantic mapping layer already decouples a Semantics name from its physical column per source: one pooled `customer_id` dimension realises as `customer_id` on the orders source and `id` on the customers source. What a name list costs is the ability to relate two *distinct* Semantics without first collapsing them into one pooled dimension, which promotes "the basis is a shared pooled Semantics" from convention to requirement.

Entries are ordered and stored as a **vector, not a set**, because the manifest side is a positional vector and wire order is positional. A repeated name is a hard error, deliberately diverging from the set semantics chosen for keyset membership, where membership is a set by nature and no order survives.

**Both sides must resolve to the same Semantics id, and that is checked.** Two *inline* same-named dimensions in different DataKinds draw no diagnostic today — the shadow check fires only against the root pool — so without this check a name list would silently join two unrelated homonyms. Id-coincidence *is* the collision check, bought for one comparison; the comparable name-list system in the wild does none at all.

Enforcement is compile-side rather than model-side, at the per-interface registry lookup, because a composite DataKind's exposed-semantics set is not known until interfaces are built, so a model-side check would have to duplicate a derivation that does not exist. The accepted cost is worse locations: parse and validate stay silent, so an editor sees no diagnostic for an unknown or mistyped join key.

**Enforcement lands compile-side but does not run there yet — the fourth disclosed dormant lane.** Relationship resolution is unwired in v1 (`manifest.relationships` stayed empty at the time of the decision), so no `compile()` path calls the resolver that holds the four rules: they are written and unit-tested against the resolver directly, and a model with a bogus join key compiled clean then. Wiring them in is deliberately *not* part of this decision, because that would change v1 behaviour; what is recorded is the rule and its home, plus the fact that the home is not yet reached. It is stated here and at the function: an unreachable check that reads as enforced is exactly the dead-lane class this work exists to remove; the rules fire when relationship lowering lands, with no further work on them.

Four typed kinds carry the rules:

- unknown-on-side, which **must** name whether `from` or `to` lacks it, because those are different authoring mistakes;
- not-a-Dimension, carrying the role it resolved to (`Field` is excluded from authoring but stays reachable in the manifest for synthesised and derived cases);
- id-mismatch, carrying both ids;
- duplicated.

They are `CompileError` variants rather than model-side `ValidateErrorKind` ones: ADR 0004 makes diagnostic identity *per-stage*, and four variants the validate stage could never produce would be a dead lane of exactly the class this work exists to remove.

`on: []` is a hard **parse** error whose diagnostic names `cross`. That makes an empty derived basis unrepresentable rather than merely rejected, and it keeps the earlier-rule supersession narrow: non-empty survives *inside* the declared arm. The Rust builder can spell what the wire form cannot, so `.build()` refuses both declared-basis rejections — the empty list and the repeated name — because otherwise a code-built model would re-serialise to a document no parser accepts. That argument admits no third case, so the build-time set is exactly those two. The build-time kinds are **validate**-stage, not the parse ones: diagnostic identity is per-stage (ADR 0004) and nothing parsed at `.build()`, so the same rule is restated at the stage that reaches it rather than borrowed from the one that does not.

**`on: cross` permits `filter:`, and that pair is the sanctioned non-equi join.** Three readings follow:

- `cross` with no filter is a true cartesian product;
- `cross` with a filter is a theta / range join, which is how `orders.order_date BETWEEN promos.start AND promos.end` becomes spellable;
- a name list with a filter is an equi-join plus residual predicate, unchanged.

So **`cross` means "no dimension-name basis", not "unconditional"**. This recovers non-equi joins that a names-only grammar would otherwise leave unspellable, at zero implementation cost, since both fields already exist and are independent. The standing non-recommendation against writing an equi-condition into `filter:` narrows accordingly to equi-conditions *under a declared or derived basis*, and stays a non-recommendation rather than a validation rule. Nothing here has a plan-IR landing site yet: cross join is not a v1 `JoinNode` variant and the `JoinNode.residual` field the theta join needs is deferred as MINOR, so `cross` is authorable and un-lowerable today. That is disclosed at the type, not discovered later.

**Permission for `auto` rests on the structural argument alone.** ADR 0028 lists "implicit join/path inference for cross-kind names" as a rejected alternative, and that rejection is **scoped, not superseded**: it ranges over *inventing an edge where none is declared*, not over *deriving a declared edge's basis*. The scoping is forced by the design's own structure rather than chosen for convenience: the ratified four-hop implicit composition (`MAX_IMPLICIT_COMPOSITION_DEPTH = 4`) is itself path inference over declared edges, so a reading of 0028 that barred all inference would already contradict a ratified feature. The argument deliberately does **not** rest on opt-in: absence of `on:` defaults to `Auto`, so derivation *is* implicit for a relationship that states no basis, and an opt-in defence would be false. How a basis is actually derived — the strategy ladder, its tie-breaks, its home and its failure diagnostic — is out of scope; this lands only the grammar in which `Auto` is spellable. Drift is likewise disclosed and unguarded: no mechanism fires when a derived basis changes, on the precedent that a wrong cardinality already yields inflated sums with no warning. This records the tolerance and claims no guard.

**The earlier non-empty-`keys` rule is superseded outright.** The earlier "`keys: Vec<KeyPair>` — non-empty" rule and its `COMP_E_0413 RelationshipEmptyKeys` no longer describe the field — which is now spelled `on:` — because it is no longer a `Vec` at all, and its empty state has been replaced by two named ones. The supersession takes the precedent's own form: non-empty survives inside the `Declared` arm, so nothing the rule protected is lost. One consequence is handed forward rather than swallowed: the foreign-key declaration retires with no successor, so the earlier rule that implicit composition "consults foreign keys when author-declared `relationships:` are absent" loses its only input and cannot be resurrected. Implicit composition walks declared relationships or nothing.

### Cardinality

`cardinality` is **conditional on the basis**. `Relationship.cardinality` is `Option<Cardinality>`, skipped on serialisation when absent, and omitting it is legal when and only when the basis is `cross`: a bare `on: cross` relationship is fully specified, while omitting it under a declared or derived basis is an error carrying the relationship name.

The substantive argument is untouched: cardinality and keyness are claims about *different facts*. The earlier composition design already says so — cardinality declares the multiplicity of the **join**, not the uniqueness of any side's keys — so keysets arriving in the manifest demote nothing, and cardinality is not inferred from them. What changed is narrower. A cross join has no dimension-name basis at all; that is what `cross` means, so demanding a cardinality beside it asks the author to state something the join's own shape already settles, and the honest answer for a conditionless join is the longest spelling to type. Stating it explicitly stays legal everywhere: all four cardinalities remain legal on `on: cross`, and a conditionless join against a single-row config table keeps its honest `many_to_one` rather than being inflated to many-to-many.

**`Option<Cardinality>` was rejected here, and is reopened.** The rejection was recorded "with no door held open", reasoning that making the field optional later is authoring-compatible and costs only a Rust API break, so holding a door bought nothing and would leave three consumers reading a `None`. The first half of that was right, and is what makes this reversal cheap; what it did not anticipate is a *rule* conditioning the absence on one basis state, which is what turns the `Option` from an unhonourable `None` into a stated condition. The reversal is narrow by construction: cardinality's three consumers past the preserved-side tag — `JoinNode.cardinality`, which is never elided; the fan-out-safe rewrite together with the Joinset `cardinality_profile` fold; and the adapter's `DISTINCT` / array-aggregation posture — still read a concrete value, because no `Option` survives compile. The neighbouring rejections stand on their own reasoning, unchanged: no fifth `Cartesian` variant, and not the `crossed` spelling.

**The cardinality matrix gains a row, and the divergence from the earlier table is deliberate.** An absent cardinality resolves to `ManyToMany` with `optional: None` and `cross_filter: None`, deriving an inner join. Both are the honest reading of a basis-less join rather than a conservative guess: a true cartesian product leaves no row unmatched, so inner is *exact*; and with no shared basis, filtering one side does not constrain the other, so no propagation is the only truthful default — which also satisfies the rule that a many-to-many relationship takes no directional cross-filter. Both stay overridable when authored, as every other cell in the matrix is. The row keys on **cardinality being absent**, not on `on: cross`, which keeps the matrix one-dimensional. The accepted consequence is that a bare `on: cross` is complete while `on: cross` with an *explicit* symmetric cardinality still owes `optional:` and `cross_filter:` under the unchanged rule that a symmetric cardinality owes both: an explicit symmetric claim owes its explicit consequences. The row lands in the code's transcription of the table under its own heading, keeping the quotation and the extension visibly separate, and is recorded here.

**The rule that a missing cardinality is refused unless the basis is `cross` moves from parse to validate, and the move is forced rather than chosen.** With the field non-`Option`, an omitted `cardinality:` produced a serde missing-field error that the parse classifier lifted into `ParseErrorKind::RelationshipMissingCardinality`. Making the field `Option` deletes that producer outright, and the mechanism could not have expressed the new rule anyway: a serde missing-field error fires before any sibling key is known, so it can never consult `on`. The parse variant therefore retires along with its classifier arm, and a validate-stage variant replaces it — restated rather than borrowed, because diagnostic identity is per-stage (ADR 0004). The rule lands beside the symmetric-cardinality rule in the relationship validation walk, which already covers root-level and joinset-local relationships. It is restated at `RelationshipBuilder::build()` for the same reason the two declared-basis rejections are: `Option` makes bon's setter optional, so `build()` becomes the only guard the code path has. The move is a gain in fidelity: the parse variant hardcoded `relationship: "<unknown>"` because serde does not surface which struct was decoding, whereas the validate walk carries the real name. One consequence is handed forward rather than swallowed: a consumer that decodes an authoring model without running validation now accepts a document the full pipeline refuses. That is the posture every other validate-stage rule already takes.

**Keysets inherit the uniqueness home.** The ratified `PLAN_W_0503 RelationshipCardinalityKeyMismatch` advisory is **rewired, not retired**: its `inferred_uniqueness` input is respecified as keyset-sourced. The check itself stays plan-stage, warning-severity and **unbuilt**. Two facts are recorded for whoever builds it: severity is free at either stage, and compile is now a viable home because both inputs are in the manifest, but the ratified stage is not moved here. Two claims in its earlier specification were stale and are corrected here: the carrier-agnostic **Constraint** DSL — the shape of a `constraints:` block — is not a key-uniqueness section; and `Key::Primary` / `Key::Unique` never existed as live types, the spelling having been `Keys.primary` / `Keys.unique` on the retired per-DataKind key block.

**The evidence rule.** A side is *claimed unique* on a declared basis when **some keyset of its DataKind has members ⊆ that basis**. The relation is `⊆`, and it is deliberately the **opposite** of the derivation-side finding, where only member-set *equality* makes a basis unique on both sides and proper containment collapses into a weaker rung. They are stated together so they are not confused: *derivation* asks whether a candidate basis is exactly a keyset (equality); the *advisory* asks whether a keyset is contained in the basis the author already wrote (subset). Subset is the right direction for the advisory because a basis wider than a keyset still joins on a unique arrangement — the extra terms only narrow — whereas a basis narrower than every keyset evidences nothing.

**Absence is never evidence.** A DataKind with no keyset has made no claim, not a claim of non-uniqueness; the advisory may fire on a contradiction between two present claims and never on a missing one. This is a general principle, not a local convenience, and it kills one tempting tie: `Integrity` is **orthogonal** to keysets. Integrity is a totality claim — every `from` row finds a `to` row — while a keyset is an injectivity claim about the `to` side; the two constrain different things. The only rule that would have tied them ("`Enforced` integrity implies the referenced side is unique") has to read a keyset's *absence* to bite, so it dies on the principle rather than on a preference.

### Amends

- **0028:** the rejected "implicit join/path inference for cross-kind names" is scoped, not superseded: it ranges over inventing an edge where none is declared, not over deriving a declared edge's basis (`on: auto`).

## More Information

### Open questions

**The fuller compile-stage cardinality validation is deferred, not declined.** A compile-stage home is unreachable today — relationship resolution is dormant, `manifest.relationships` stays empty in v1, and every relationship function in the seal pass is dead-code-allowed and live only via tests — so building one would open a further dormant lane of exactly the class the join-key rules above already disclose. Two facts are recorded for whoever picks it up. First, the defaults matrix's absent-cardinality row trusts validate, in the same way the symmetric arm already trusts the symmetric-cardinality rule: nothing at the matrix re-checks that the basis was `cross`. Second, one gap is accepted rather than patched — the many-to-many cross-filter rule keys on the *authored* cardinality, so a relationship with cardinality absent and `cross_filter: left` authored draws no diagnostic, yet the matrix then resolves cardinality to `ManyToMany`, a pair that rule would forbid had it been authored. Inventing a validate-stage rule for it would be a guess about a compile-stage fact. The real question is where cardinality's post-resolution invariants live, and it is left open rather than answered by default.
